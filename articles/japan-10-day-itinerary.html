Japan 10-Day Itinerary: Tokyo, Kyoto & Osaka

A balanced first-time route through Japan’s best-known urban and cultural stops, with time for neighbourhoods, food and day trips instead of racing between distant regions.

TRAVORA Editorial · Updated November 27, 2026 · 10 days
At a glance: Tokyo (3 nights) → Kyoto (4 nights) → Osaka (2 nights). This route assumes arrival in Tokyo and departure from the Kansai area. Reverse it if your flights are cheaper or more convenient in the opposite direction.

Before you book: make the route work

Ten days is enough for a rewarding first visit if you focus on the Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka corridor. The itinerary below includes Nara as a day trip from Kyoto. It deliberately leaves out Hokkaido, Okinawa and far-western Japan; adding them would mean more transfers and less time in each place.

Book accommodation near a useful station, but compare the exact neighbourhood rather than choosing only by distance to a major landmark. Check airport transfer options, the last train time, luggage rules and whether your hotel can store bags before check-in.

Your 10-day Japan itinerary

Day 1: Arrive in Tokyo

Keep the first day light. Travel from the airport to your accommodation, get a local transport card or app set up, and explore the neighbourhood around your hotel. Avoid planning a timed attraction immediately after a long international flight.

Day 2: Asakusa, Ueno and old Tokyo

Start around Sensō-ji and the surrounding streets, then spend the afternoon in Ueno if museums, parks or markets interest you. Group nearby stops together and check museum opening days before travelling.

Day 3: Shibuya, Harajuku and Shinjuku

Explore the west side of the city. Walk between Harajuku and Omotesando, visit Shibuya, then choose an evening viewpoint, food area or quieter local neighbourhood. Reserve popular timed-entry attractions ahead when required.

Day 4: Travel to Kyoto

Take a Shinkansen to Kyoto and leave the first half of the day flexible for the transfer. After check-in, explore Nishiki Market or the Gion and Kamo River area. Compare point-to-point fares with rail-pass options using your actual route before purchasing a pass.

Day 5: Higashiyama and eastern Kyoto

Visit the Kiyomizu-dera area and walk through the historic lanes nearby. Go early to reduce crowd pressure, respect residential spaces and follow photography signs. Add a garden or temple only if the pace feels comfortable.

Day 6: Fushimi Inari or Arashiyama

Choose one side of Kyoto rather than trying to cross the city repeatedly. Fushimi Inari works well for an early walk; Arashiyama suits visitors who want riverside scenery and gardens. Check local bus and rail routes before leaving.

Day 7: Nara day trip

Travel from Kyoto to Nara for the park, temple precincts and historic centre. Keep a respectful distance from wildlife and follow posted feeding rules. Return to Kyoto for the evening or move your luggage to Osaka if that makes the next day easier.

Day 8: Osaka and Dōtonbori

Move to Osaka, settle in and explore the Namba and Dōtonbori area. Try regional food, but treat famous restaurants as optional rather than building the whole day around queues. Choose accommodation with a convenient route to your next day's plans.

Day 9: Osaka at your pace

Choose Osaka Castle and nearby parks, an aquarium, a museum or a booked theme-park day. Check official attraction calendars and ticket rules before travelling. Keep the evening open for a final meal or shopping.

Day 10: Depart from Kansai

Allow enough time to reach Kansai International Airport or your departure station. Check your airline’s terminal, check-in deadline and the current airport rail or bus timetable. If you fly home from Tokyo, add the return transfer time and consider whether an open-jaw ticket is worth comparing.

How much time should you spend in each city?

  • Tokyo: three nights gives you two full sightseeing days and a gentle arrival.
  • Kyoto: four nights allows two city days, one flexible day and the Nara excursion.
  • Osaka: two nights gives you one full day and a practical departure base.

If you prefer slower travel, spend an extra night in Kyoto or Tokyo and shorten Osaka. If food and nightlife are your main interests, move one night from Kyoto to Osaka.

Transport: rail passes, luggage and local travel

Do not assume a national Japan Rail Pass saves money. Add up the long-distance journeys you will actually take, compare the current pass price and coverage, and remember that some local railways and metro systems are operated by other companies. Regional passes may suit a different route better.

Reserve seats when your travel date, route or luggage makes it sensible. Check current oversized-baggage rules for the trains you plan to use. For city travel, use the official operator information for fares, service changes and station access. Leave a buffer around airport transfers and major intercity journeys.

When to take this trip

Spring blossom periods and autumn foliage are popular, but exact timing changes by year and region. Summer can be hot and humid in major cities; winter is colder in Tokyo and Kyoto, while mountain regions may have snow and different transport conditions. Check forecasts and seasonal notices close to departure, and reserve high-demand accommodation early when travelling during holidays.

Practical booking checklist

  • Confirm entry requirements for your passport using official Japanese government sources.
  • Compare flights into Tokyo and out of Osaka/Kansai, as well as a return flight from Tokyo.
  • Choose accommodation close to the stations and neighbourhoods you will actually use.
  • Check current train fares, pass coverage, seat reservations and baggage rules.
  • Book timed-entry attractions only after confirming your dates and cancellation terms.
  • Arrange mobile data if needed, checking eSIM compatibility and coverage for your device.
  • Keep travel insurance, emergency contacts and key bookings available offline.
