The Practical Carry-On Packing Checklist for International Travel

A well-packed carry-on makes the first day of a trip easier and gives you a backup if checked baggage is delayed. The goal is not to pack everything: it is to keep essential, difficult-to-replace items accessible while respecting airline and border rules.

Before you travel: Entry rules, opening hours, transport services, prices and safety conditions can change. Confirm time-sensitive details with official local sources before making bookings.

Keep essential documents accessible

Pack your passport and any required visas, travel authorisations, insurance details, accommodation information and onward-travel documents in a secure, easy-to-reach place. Keep digital copies in a secure location, but do not rely on a phone alone.

Check passport validity and entry requirements for every country on your itinerary, including transit stops. Some destinations apply different rules depending on nationality, route or document type.

Pack medication and health essentials carefully

Keep necessary prescription medicines in original packaging where practical, along with a copy of the prescription or a clinician’s letter when appropriate. Check the destination’s rules for controlled or restricted medicines before departure; a medicine permitted at home may be regulated elsewhere.

Bring a small set of personal essentials such as glasses, contact-lens supplies, basic first-aid items and any medically necessary equipment. Confirm airline requirements for batteries, medical devices and liquids in advance.

Build a small clothing system

Choose versatile layers that can be mixed and matched. A practical starting point is a change of underwear and socks, one spare top, a light layer and sleepwear if space allows. Adjust for climate, trip length, laundry access and planned activities.

Wear your bulkiest comfortable shoes and jacket during transit. Pack a compact bag for day trips if it will be useful, but avoid carrying items you will not use.

Organise electronics and liquids

Keep charging cables, a compatible power adapter, headphones and a power bank together. Check airline rules for spare lithium batteries and power banks, including capacity limits and whether they must travel in cabin baggage.

Liquid restrictions differ by airport and country. Check the departure and transit airport rules for container sizes, transparent bags and exemptions. Do not assume that a rule at one airport applies everywhere.

Make the flight more comfortable

A refillable empty water bottle, eye mask, earplugs, light scarf and a few snacks can make a long journey easier. Fill the bottle after security where facilities allow. Choose items that are useful rather than bulky.

Keep valuables and anything you would need during a delay in your personal item. Do not place passports, medication or essential electronics in checked baggage.

Check baggage rules before leaving home

Airlines set their own cabin-bag dimensions, weight limits and personal-item rules, and these can differ by fare type and aircraft. Measure your bag when packed and check the operating carrier’s latest policy for every flight on a multi-airline itinerary.

Review customs rules for food, plants, animal products, cash declarations and restricted goods at your destination. A packing checklist is a planning aid, not permission to carry an item across a border.
