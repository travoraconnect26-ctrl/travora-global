Italy Travel Guide

Italy's regions have distinct histories, landscapes and food traditions. The major art cities are only one part of the country: lakes, mountains, islands, rural landscapes and smaller towns offer very different journeys.

Build a regional itinerary

Rome is a major base for ancient history, museums and neighbourhood food. Florence offers Renaissance art and access to Tuscany; Venice has a distinctive lagoon setting; and Naples is a gateway to Campania and the archaeological sites around the Bay of Naples. The Dolomites, Italian lakes, Puglia, Sicily and Sardinia each reward a more focused trip.

Rail, roads and local transport

High-speed trains connect many major cities, while regional trains serve smaller destinations. Book popular routes in advance and check whether a ticket requires validation or a seat reservation. Driving can be useful in rural areas, but historic centres often have restricted traffic zones; research parking and ZTL rules before entering a town by car.

Food is regional

Look beyond familiar dishes: pasta shapes, breads, cheeses, seafood and seasonal recipes vary considerably. Ask about local specialities and ingredients, especially if you have dietary needs. Markets and neighbourhood trattorias can offer a more local perspective than restaurants beside major sights.

Timing and crowds

Spring and autumn are often useful seasons for city sightseeing, while summer is busy in many coastal and cultural destinations. Heat, mountain weather and ferry schedules vary by region. Reserve timed-entry attractions and accommodation early during major holidays and peak periods.

A realistic first visit

With about a week, choose two or three connected stops such as Rome and Florence, with a day trip if time allows. Add Venice or a southern region on a longer itinerary rather than trying to cover the entire peninsula at speed.

Before you go

Check current entry rules, official museum ticket channels, train schedules and local transport notices. Confirm any restricted-area driving rules with the relevant municipality.
