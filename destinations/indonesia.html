Indonesia Travel Guide

Indonesia is an immense archipelago with major differences between islands. Bali is only one part of the country: Java's cities and volcanoes, Sumatra's forests, Sulawesi's marine life and the eastern islands each involve different travel logistics and cultural contexts.

Indonesia is an immense archipelago with major differences between islands. Bali is only one part of the country: Java's cities and volcanoes, Sumatra's forests, Sulawesi's marine life and the eastern islands each involve different travel logistics and cultural contexts.

Think in islands, not just attractions

Bali combines Hindu cultural traditions, beaches, arts and inland landscapes, but popular areas can be congested. Java offers major cities, temples and volcanic scenery. Sumatra is known for rainforest landscapes and lake regions; Sulawesi has distinctive highland and marine environments; and eastern islands reward travellers with more time and careful logistics.

Island-hopping logistics

Domestic flights and ferries connect the archipelago, but schedules, ports and weather conditions vary. Confirm the exact departure harbour, baggage rules and transfer time. Avoid tight connections between separate tickets, and keep a buffer day when a ferry or flight is essential to the next leg.

Volcanoes, nature and safety

Volcanic areas can close at short notice because of activity, weather or conservation rules. Use authorised local guides where required, follow official exclusion zones and never treat an online trail description as confirmation that a route is open. Choose wildlife experiences that avoid handling or disturbing animals.

Culture and responsible travel

Indonesia is culturally and religiously diverse. Dress respectfully at temples and places of worship, ask before photographing people and follow local guidance during ceremonies. Reduce plastic waste, respect marine protected areas and choose operators with clear environmental practices.

A practical first route

A shorter first visit should focus on one island or a simple two-island combination with reliable connections. Java and Bali can form a route for travellers interested in cities, temples and varied landscapes, but allow sufficient time for overland journeys and ferry transfers.

Before you go

Visa and entry conditions depend on nationality and may change. Check official Indonesian immigration information, local volcano and weather advisories, ferry operator updates and any park or permit requirements before departure.

Travel information: Entry rules, prices, opening times, safety conditions, transport schedules and access can change. Confirm details with official government, transport and destination sources before departure.
