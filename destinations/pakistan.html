Pakistan Travel Guide

Pakistan spans the Arabian Sea, the Indus plains and some of the world's highest mountain ranges. Its regions offer very different journeys: historic cities and Mughal architecture, archaeological heritage, coastal landscapes and high-altitude valleys. Pakistan spans the Arabian Sea, the Indus plains and some of the world's highest mountain ranges. Its regions offer very different journeys: historic cities and Mughal architecture, archaeological heritage, coastal landscapes and high-altitude valleys. Distances, weather and local conditions make careful route planning essential.

Where to go

Lahore is a strong choice for Mughal-era architecture, the Walled City, museums and Punjabi food. Islamabad offers museums, green spaces and a convenient northern gateway. Hunza and Skardu are major bases for Karakoram scenery, mountain lakes and trekking, while Karachi combines a large coastal city with varied food traditions. Mohenjo-daro and Makli offer important archaeological and historic context.

Planning the north

Do not treat Hunza, Gilgit and Skardu as quick side trips from the capital. Mountain journeys can take longer than expected, and road access may be affected by weather, landslides or seasonal closures. Build buffer days into the itinerary, use experienced local drivers or guides for remote routes and check current road and weather information before departure.

Getting around

Domestic flights can connect major cities with northern gateways, but weather disruptions are possible. Long-distance buses serve many urban routes. Mountain roads are slower and more exposed to changing conditions than maps suggest. Avoid tight connections and confirm departure points, luggage rules and cancellation arrangements with transport operators.

Food and culture

Food is regional: Lahore is known for Punjabi dishes and food streets; Karachi reflects Sindhi, coastal and many migrant traditions; Peshawar has a strong meat and bread culture; northern areas feature local breads, soups, apricots and dairy. Dress and behave with sensitivity to local customs, ask before photographing people and respect religious sites.

A manageable first trip

A city-and-heritage route such as Islamabad–Lahore can work well for a shorter visit. A dedicated northern trip should focus on one area, such as Hunza or Skardu, and allow enough time for altitude adjustment and delays. Combining far-apart regions in a short holiday usually creates more transit than meaningful exploration.

Before you go

Visa and entry rules depend on nationality and can change; verify through official Pakistani immigration and e-visa channels. Check current government travel advisories, local road authority updates, weather services, permits where applicable and available health facilities. This guide does not confirm present safety or access.

Travel information: Entry rules, prices, opening times, safety conditions, transport schedules and access can change. Confirm details with official government, transport and destination sources before departure.
