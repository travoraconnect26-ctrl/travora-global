United States Travel Guide

The United States is too large to treat as one compact itinerary. A satisfying trip usually focuses on one region or a small cluster of nearby states, balancing travel time with the cities, landscapes or experiences that matter most to you.

Home / Country guides / United States

Choose a region first

For a first city-focused trip, the Northeast corridor links places such as New York, Philadelphia, Washington, D.C. and Boston. The West Coast can combine coastal cities and national parks, but distances are substantial. The Southwest is known for desert landscapes and canyon country; the Pacific Northwest mixes cities, forests and coastline; the South offers distinct music, food and history.

Getting around

Domestic flights are often the practical choice between distant regions. Amtrak serves selected intercity corridors, with especially useful services in the Northeast, while local transit varies widely by city. A rental car offers flexibility for national parks and rural routes, but parking, tolls, long driving distances and one-way rental fees can add cost.

When to go

There is no single best season for the entire country. Spring and autumn can be comfortable in many temperate regions, while summer suits some northern and mountain destinations but brings heat and crowds elsewhere. Winter conditions vary from mild coastal areas to severe snow and mountain weather. Check regional forecasts and park alerts.

Food and regional culture

Food traditions change markedly across the country: seafood in coastal regions, barbecue in parts of the South, Southwestern and Mexican-influenced cooking in the Southwest, and immigrant food cultures in major cities. Local festivals, museums and neighbourhoods can add context beyond headline attractions.

A realistic first route

For 8–10 days, choose one corridor such as New York–Philadelphia–Washington, D.C., or a single region such as California. Avoid combining the East and West Coasts in a short trip unless flights are a deliberate part of the plan.

Before you travel

Check the entry route that applies to your nationality, including visa or travel-authorization requirements, and review state and park rules. Budget for taxes, tips, insurance, domestic transport and possible resort or parking fees. National park reservations and seasonal road closures can affect plans.

Plan with current information.

Entry rules, transport schedules, weather and local conditions change. Verify details with official government, transport and destination sources before booking or departure.
