Japan Travel Guide

Japan rewards a trip built around contrast: energetic city neighbourhoods, quiet temple precincts, mountain towns, coastal landscapes and regional food traditions. Rather than rushing across the archipelago, choose a route that fits your available days and the season.

Japan rewards a trip built around contrast: energetic city neighbourhoods, quiet temple precincts, mountain towns, coastal landscapes and regional food traditions. Rather than rushing across the archipelago, choose a route that fits your available days and the season.

Where to go

For a first visit, Tokyo offers neighbourhood variety, museums, food and easy day trips. Kyoto is known for temples, gardens and traditional streets; nearby Osaka brings a lively food scene, while Nara makes a manageable heritage excursion. Beyond this classic corridor, consider the Japanese Alps for mountain scenery, Hokkaido for wide landscapes and winter activities, Kyushu for hot springs and volcanic terrain, or Okinawa for subtropical island culture.

Getting around

Rail is the backbone of many independent itineraries. Shinkansen connect major cities, while local rail and metro systems cover urban areas. Compare the current cost of individual tickets with regional or national passes using your exact route; a pass is not automatically the cheapest option. IC cards simplify many local journeys. Rural buses and ferries may be infrequent, so check schedules before committing to a day trip.

When to visit

Spring blossoms and autumn foliage are popular, but timing varies by region and year. Summer can be hot and humid, while northern and mountain regions have distinct snow seasons. Check local forecasts and seasonal conditions for each stop rather than relying on a single nationwide season description.

Food and local culture

Try regional specialities rather than treating Japanese cuisine as one menu: ramen styles, soba, sushi, okonomiyaki, seafood and seasonal sweets vary by place. Learn basic dining and public-transport etiquette, keep voices low in quiet spaces, follow photography signs and respect shrine, temple and bathing customs.

A realistic first itinerary

With roughly 7–10 days, focus on Tokyo and the Kansai region instead of adding distant destinations. A longer trip can add a mountain or rural stop. Reserve accommodation early during major holiday periods, keep luggage manageable on trains and check oversized-baggage rules where they apply.

Before you go

Visa requirements depend on passport and can change. Confirm entry rules through official Japanese government sources, check transport operators for current timetables and fares, and monitor official weather and disaster notices. This guide is planning context, not a live confirmation of access or conditions.
