United Kingdom Travel Guide

The United Kingdom rewards a regional approach. London is a major gateway, but Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland and England’s historic cities offer very different landscapes and travel rhythms. Choose fewer bases and allow time for the distances between them.

Home / Country guides / United Kingdom

Where to focus

For a first visit, combine London with one additional region rather than trying to cover the whole country. York and the North of England add historic streets and rail connections; Edinburgh and the Scottish Highlands offer castles, cities and dramatic scenery; Wales is a strong choice for coast, mountains and smaller towns; Northern Ireland pairs Belfast with the Antrim Coast.

Getting around

Intercity rail is useful between major cities, especially on the London–Midlands–North England corridor and routes to Scotland and Wales. Book popular long-distance services ahead where practical. Local buses, regional trains and rental cars fill gaps; a car is often more useful for rural routes than for city centres. Remember that driving is on the left.

When to go

Late spring to early autumn generally brings longer daylight and more options for walking and rural travel, though weather remains changeable. Summer is busiest at major attractions and in popular countryside areas. Autumn can suit city breaks and quieter routes; winter days are short, particularly in Scotland and northern regions.

Food and local character

Explore the differences between regions: pub and market food, afternoon tea traditions, Welsh produce, Scottish seafood and whisky regions, and Northern Irish baking and local produce. Large cities are highly international, while smaller towns and rural areas have their own strong identities.

A realistic first route

Allow 7–10 days for London (3–4 nights), York or another northern city (1–2 nights), and Edinburgh (2–3 nights). For a longer trip, add the Lake District, Wales or Northern Ireland rather than rushing through all four nations.

Before you travel

The UK is made up of four nations with different local transport networks and regional experiences. Check current entry permission, passport rules, rail engineering works, attraction booking requirements and weather alerts using official sources before departure.

Plan with current information.

Entry rules, transport schedules, weather and local conditions change. Verify details with official government, transport and destination sources before booking or departure.
