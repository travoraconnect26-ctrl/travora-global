Australia Travel Guide

Australia’s size shapes every itinerary. Sydney, Melbourne, the Great Barrier Reef, the Red Centre and the west coast are not quick side trips from one another. Choose a region first, then build a route around realistic flight or driving times.

Home / Country guides / Australia

Where to focus

A first visit might centre on Sydney and nearby coastal or mountain areas, Melbourne and Victoria’s food and coastal routes, or Queensland’s reef and tropical north. The Red Centre around Uluru is a distinct inland journey; Western Australia offers vast distances and extraordinary coastlines. Tasmania suits a slower nature and road-trip focus.

Getting around

Domestic flights are often the most time-efficient way to connect distant regions. Trains serve selected corridors, while rental cars are useful for regional touring and national parks. Long-distance driving can be tiring: plan fuel stops, check road conditions and avoid driving unfamiliar outback routes after dark, when wildlife risks can be higher.

Seasons and climate

Australia spans tropical, desert, temperate and alpine climates, so seasons differ by region. The tropical north has wet and dry seasons; southern cities have cooler winters; inland areas can experience extreme heat. Check local seasonal conditions rather than relying on a single nationwide weather assumption.

Nature and local experiences

Coastal walks, reefs, rainforests, wildlife encounters, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultural experiences, food regions and city neighbourhoods can all shape a trip. Choose licensed, respectful cultural experiences and follow local guidance around wildlife and protected environments.

A realistic first route

For 8–12 days, focus on Sydney and one nearby region, or Melbourne and Victoria. If the Great Barrier Reef is a priority, build a separate Queensland itinerary with enough time for weather flexibility. Add the Red Centre only when flights and several nights fit comfortably.

Before you travel

Check current visa requirements, biosecurity and customs declarations, state road rules, park access and seasonal warnings. Australia has strict biosecurity controls. In remote areas, carry water, plan fuel and communications, and follow official advice about heat, fire, floods, marine stingers and surf conditions.

Plan with current information.

Entry rules, transport schedules, weather and local conditions change. Verify details with official government, transport and destination sources before booking or departure.
