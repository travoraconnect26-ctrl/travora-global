France Travel Guide

France is best understood region by region. Paris and its museums are only one part of the country: Normandy's coast and history, Alpine landscapes, Atlantic surf towns, Mediterranean cities, vineyards and rural villages all create different kinds of trips.

Choose a region before you choose a route

Paris is a natural first stop for museums, architecture, food and major transport links. Normandy adds coastal scenery and historic sites; the Loire Valley is known for châteaux and river landscapes; Provence and the Côte d’Azur offer Mediterranean towns and countryside; the Alps suit mountain activities; and Brittany has a distinctive Atlantic coast and food culture.

Getting around

France has an extensive rail network, including high-speed TGV services between major cities. Regional trains and buses connect smaller centres, though rural schedules can be limited. Compare train, coach and car options for each leg; a car can help in the countryside but is often unnecessary in large cities. Reserve popular long-distance trains ahead where possible.

Food and local culture

Regional food is central to the experience, from Normandy dairy and seafood to Lyonnaise cooking, Provençal markets, Alsatian specialities and Basque cuisine. Learn a few polite French phrases, respect meal customs and check local opening days because smaller shops and restaurants may close at different times.

When to visit

Spring and autumn can be comfortable for city and countryside travel, while summer brings high demand in many destinations. Mountain conditions vary by altitude and season. Check local event calendars and weather for the specific region rather than assuming conditions are the same nationwide.

A manageable first itinerary

For a first 7–10 day trip, combine Paris with one nearby region such as Normandy or the Loire Valley, or pair Paris with a second major city by train. A longer trip can focus on Provence, the Alps or the southwest. Avoid crossing the whole country in a short visit.

Before you go

Entry requirements depend on nationality and may change. Confirm visa and Schengen conditions through official sources, and check rail operators for current timetables, fares and strike or disruption notices.
